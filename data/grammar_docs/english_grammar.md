# Subject-Verb Agreement
The subject and verb must agree in number. A singular subject takes a singular verb; a plural subject takes a plural verb.
- Correct: She goes to school. / They go to school.
- Wrong: She go to school. / They goes to school.

# Past Simple vs Present Perfect
Use past simple for completed actions at a specific time. Use present perfect for actions with a connection to the present.
- Past simple: I visited Paris in 2019.
- Present perfect: I have visited Paris (at some point in my life).

# Articles: a, an, the
Use "a" before consonant sounds, "an" before vowel sounds. Use "the" for specific nouns already known to both speaker and listener.
- a cat, an apple, the sun
- Wrong: I saw a sun today. / Correct: I saw the sun today.

# Conditional Sentences
Type 1 (real/possible): If + present simple, will + infinitive. If it rains, I will stay home.
Type 2 (unreal/hypothetical): If + past simple, would + infinitive. If I had money, I would travel.
Type 3 (unreal past): If + past perfect, would have + past participle. If I had studied, I would have passed.

# Countable vs Uncountable Nouns
Countable nouns can be singular or plural: a book, two books. Uncountable nouns have no plural form: water, information, advice.
- Wrong: I need an advice. / Correct: I need advice.
- Wrong: She gave me many informations. / Correct: She gave me a lot of information.

# Prepositions of Time: in, on, at
- at: specific times (at 9pm, at noon)
- on: days and dates (on Monday, on July 4th)
- in: months, years, seasons (in June, in 2024, in summer)

# Gerund vs Infinitive
Some verbs are followed by gerund (-ing), others by infinitive (to + verb).
- Gerund after: enjoy, avoid, finish, suggest. Example: I enjoy swimming.
- Infinitive after: want, decide, hope, plan. Example: I want to swim.
- Both (same meaning): like, love, hate, begin. Example: I like swimming / I like to swim.

# Passive Voice
Formed with: be + past participle. Use when the agent is unknown, unimportant, or obvious.
- Active: The chef cooked the meal.
- Passive: The meal was cooked (by the chef).

# Relative Clauses
Use "who" for people, "which" for things, "that" for both in defining clauses.
- The man who called you is my brother.
- The book that/which I read was excellent.

# Modal Verbs
- can/could: ability or possibility
- must/have to: obligation
- should: advice
- may/might: possibility
- would: conditional or polite request
Modals are always followed by bare infinitive: You should go (not "should to go").
